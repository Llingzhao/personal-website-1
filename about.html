About

A beginner's mind, a microscopist's patience

From clinical optometry to the nuclear pore — how I ended up chasing molecules through a ~50-nanometer gate.

Hi, I'm Ling Zhao.

I am a first-year PhD student in Biology at Temple University, Philadelphia, working in the Yang Lab. My scientific curiosity lives at the interface of structure and dynamics: the nuclear pore complex is one of the largest protein assemblies in the cell, yet the molecules it transports are tiny — I want to understand how such a massive, ordered scaffold manages such selective, dynamic traffic.

My route here was not a straight line. I trained in optometry, analyzed ophthalmic clinical imaging data across multiple research centers, and worked in diagnostic laboratories — learning along the way how much careful image quality, quantitative analysis, and reproducible documentation matter. Those habits now shape how I approach microscopy: I like my images honest and my measurements reproducible.

I am currently at the start of my doctoral training: taking courses, rotating toward a dissertation project, and learning the tools of the trade — fixation and labeling chemistry, super-resolution and single-molecule fluorescence microscopy, and quantitative image analysis.
